Fast to start

It's lightweight

But nowhere near barebones

There is no installer, no update prompt, and no first-run tour. You open a tab, press Ctrl/⌘+N, pick a canvas size, and you are drawing. Because the editor runs on HTML5 canvas rather than a desktop toolkit, an old laptop or a school Chromebook gets the same workspace a new workstation does.

  • Opens in seconds on modest hardware
  • Familiar Paint-style layout — no learning curve
  • Dark and light themes that remember your choice

Four steps, one strange little world — every frame is the same document, one editable layer further on.

Real compositing

Full layer support

It handles multi-layer projects without complaining

This is the line most free web editors fail to cross. Pinta Online keeps a proper layer stack: add, duplicate, merge down, reorder, hide, rename, flip, rotate, and scale, with per-layer opacity and 16 blend modes. Drop a photo onto the canvas, put your artwork above it, and adjust either one without touching the other.

  • Drag and drop files straight onto the workspace
  • Per-layer opacity, visibility, and blend mode
  • OpenRaster export keeps the stack intact for later

The full toolbox

An impressive toolkit

Everything a quick design actually needs

Rectangle, ellipse, lasso, and magic-wand selections that union, subtract, intersect, and invert. Brush, pencil, eraser, bucket, clone stamp, recolor, and color picker. Lines and curves with draggable control points, five gradient shapes, and on-canvas multiline text. The magic wand in particular is accurate enough to knock a background out on the first try.

  • Five selection tools with modifier-aware combine modes
  • Editable shapes and curves, not baked-in pixels
  • Linear, radial, conical, reflected, and diamond gradients

Not just drawing

It's a real image editor too

You never have to leave the tab to fix a photo

Curves and Levels are the actual dialogs, per channel, with spline control points. Brightness/contrast, hue and saturation, posterize, sepia, and black and white sit next to them. Then there are 55 built-in and optional effects — blurs, sharpen, artistic treatments, distortions, noise, stylize, and procedural renders like clouds and fractals — all running off the UI thread so the canvas never locks up.

  • Curves, Levels, and hue/saturation, per channel
  • 46 built-in plus 9 optional worker-backed effects
  • Every application lands in a branch-aware undo history

Before you ask

Is this online paint app really free?

Yes. Pinta Online is free and open source under the MIT X11 license. No account, no trial, no watermark, no subscription.

Do my images get uploaded to a server?

No. Files are decoded, edited, composited, and exported by your own browser. There is no upload step, and restored documents are kept locally in your browser's IndexedDB on that device.

Does it support layers?

Yes — add, duplicate, merge, reorder, hide, rename, rotate, and scale layers, with opacity and 16 blend modes. OpenRaster import and export preserve the whole stack.

Which operating systems does it run on?

Any system with a current browser: Windows, macOS, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, iPad. There is no separate download per platform.

Can I use it offline?

Yes. Install it as a Progressive Web App and the interface starts offline from cache. Opening and saving local files needs no connection.

How is it related to desktop Pinta?

Pinta Online is a browser edition built on Pinta 3.2, reusing the original artwork, shortcuts, and interaction vocabulary. The original C# and GTK desktop app is still available from the upstream Pinta project.
